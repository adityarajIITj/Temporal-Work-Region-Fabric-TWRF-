## Evidence classification

Measured simulator evidence includes execution counts, skips, version checks, dependency activity, ready-set/queue activity, State Store operations/bytes, traces, parity, and audit outcomes.

Derived architectural evidence consists of cycle estimates generated by applying declared timing parameters to measured simulator counts.

Estimated evidence consists of prospective RTL/FPGA mappings before physical synthesis.

Unsupported claims include physical GPU speed, FPS, energy, power, silicon area, FPGA timing closure, and production-scale workload performance.

## Publication rules

1. Never describe modeled cycles as physical GPU measurements.
2. Never convert modeled cost differences into FPS or speedup claims.
3. Do not claim universal superiority.
4. Do not claim individual mechanisms such as memoization, dependency tracking, spatial tiling, or temporal reuse as novel.
5. Preserve the distinction between raster quantitative evidence and cross-workload architectural evidence.
6. Regenerate machine-readable artifacts from the frozen source before submission.
7. Verify bibliographic metadata against final publisher records before external submission.

## Final statement

TWRF provides a reproducible virtual-GPU study of persistent spatial work objects with explicit validity, dependency, state, output, and scheduling semantics. Under the declared timing model, the hardware-oriented organization has lower modeled cost than the executable software incremental baseline across the evaluated matrix and sensitivity grid. The study also identifies the management-cost boundary that prevents a universal advantage over simpler temporal caching or non-static full recomputation. Physical hardware validation remains future work.
